PUBERTY & VR — MNE MANUAL EEG REVIEW (FOUR CHANNELS)
===================================================

PURPOSE
-------
Use all four original full-rate Muse/Mind Monitor channels (TP9, AF7, AF8,
TP10) for visual artifact investigation. AF7 and AF8 remain the ONLY primary
channels for alpha (8–13 Hz), beta (13–30 Hz) and ln(beta/alpha) analysis.
TP9 and TP10 provide context; do not include them in the primary frontal ratio.

INSTALL / FIRST RUN
-------------------
Place EEG_Manual_Review.py in Puberty_VR/scripts/eeg and, in VS Code, choose
its Python environment. From the Puberty_VR project root:

  python3 -c "import mne; print(mne.__version__)"
  python3 scripts/eeg/EEG_Manual_Review.py --subject 029 --phase pre_vr

Four channels now appear in order TP9, AF7, AF8, TP10. --channel ALL is the
explicit four-channel option. The legacy --channel BOTH acts as an alias for
ALL. You can review one channel alone with --channel AF7 / AF8 / TP9 / TP10.

  python3 scripts/eeg/EEG_Manual_Review.py --subject 029 --phase pre_vr --channel ALL --start 20 --duration 12
  python3 scripts/eeg/EEG_Manual_Review.py --subject 029 --phase pre_vr --list-flags
  python3 scripts/eeg/EEG_Manual_Review.py --subject 029 --phase pre_vr --channel AF8 --start 20

The audit --list-flags feature currently lists flags only for AF7 and AF8,
even though the MNE browser displays all four channels.

HOW TO REVIEW
-------------
1. Inspect all four channels together to see whether suspicious changes happen
   at the same time and whether they are localized to a frontal sensor.
2. Look at 5–12 seconds at a time; zoom for suspicious intervals. Compare
   amplitude, waveform shape, continuity, and timing among the channels.
3. Press A in the active MNE waveform browser to enter annotation mode.
   Create/select a descriptive label, then drag over the affected time span.
4. For a disturbance affecting only AF8, run --channel AF8 to annotate it:
   the CSV/FIF from single-channel mode marks affected_channels=AF8. Repeat
   separately for AF7 as needed. Do NOT mark all channels for an AF8-only issue.
5. Annotations made in ALL view are global to the four displayed channels.
   Use ALL for truly shared disturbances or for non-excluding REVIEW labels;
   use single-channel mode for final channel-specific exclusion decisions.
6. Close the MNE window to save; re-run the same command to reopen the marks.
   Different selections (ALL, AF7, AF8, etc.) are saved in distinct files.
7. Record rationale and uncertainty in the exported CSV reviewer_note column.
   Before final analysis, a separate consolidation step must reconcile
   annotations from ALL and individual-channel review files.

ARTIFACT EXAMPLES — WHAT TO LOOK FOR AND HOW TO LABEL
----------------------------------------------------
These are visual patterns/suspected sources, NOT confirmed clinical diagnoses.
Do not exclude merely because alpha or beta changes as hypothesized.

A. BAD_flatline
   Trace becomes abnormally straight/unchanging for a sustained period;
   may mean contact failure, frozen values, or acquisition dropout. EEG has
   not 'stopped' in the brain. Mark only channels actually affected.

B. BAD_clipping_suspected
   Repeated exact ceiling/floor values or flattened tops/bottoms, often with
   sudden transitions. Can reflect saturation or device data coding. Verify
   native units and any device-specific limits before claiming saturation.
   Example to examine: subject 029 pre-VR AF8 near 22.8–23.7 seconds, where
   earlier review figures suggested plateaus near 0 and 1450; visually check
   the ORIGINAL data and refine boundaries before applying an exclusion.

C. BAD_contact_step
   Sudden, disproportionately large baseline step/pop, possibly followed by
   gradual recovery. Could be electrode contact or movement. When ambiguous,
   use REVIEW_uncertain until confirmed by inspection.

D. BAD_ocular_suspected
   Large, rounded, relatively slow frontal deflection suggestive of blink or
   eye movement. Compare AF7/AF8 and TP9/TP10; frontal prominence supports
   suspicion but is NOT proof, particularly without dedicated EOG.

E. BAD_muscle_suspected
   Sustained burst of dense, fast, irregular oscillations, possibly EMG from
   forehead, jaw or facial motion. Check context in TP9/TP10; jaw/ear-area
   muscles can contaminate them too. This matters especially for beta power.

F. BAD_motion_suspected
   Irregular high-amplitude signal disturbance across one or multiple
   channels near a likely headband motion event. Shared disturbances could
   also result from common reference effects; do not assume true movement.

G. BAD_electrical_noise
   Unusually regular, often narrow-band oscillation; check PSD for narrow
   peaks (such as 60 Hz). A 60-Hz component alone is not necessarily a reason
   to reject alpha/beta when filtering adequately suppresses it; use this
   label for severe unresolved contamination affecting the useful signal.

H. BAD_missing_raw_sample
   Missing raw samples interpolated solely for visualization. The script
   inserts these annotations automatically, channel by channel. Interpolated
   viewer values MUST NOT enter the final PSD calculations as valid EEG.

I. REVIEW_uncertain
   A pattern is suspicious but type, source, or exclusion decision is not
   justified. This label is a review queue, not an automatic rejection.
   Ask the neuroscientist collaborator for adjudication.

GENERAL DECISION RULES
----------------------
- Obvious and consequential signal loss: annotate BAD_[specific_reason].
- Plausible artifact but unclear source/extent: REVIEW_uncertain first.
- No convincing abnormality: leave unannotated; visual pass is not proof of
  artifact-free signal.
- An event in TP9 alone does not require exclusion of AF7 or AF8.
- A shared event might reflect a reference disturbance; simultaneous events
  across channels are clues, not a definitive artifact classification.
- Only exclude the period actually compromised, with sensible boundaries.
- Never 'clean' by altering original raw CSV/ZIP files.

IMPORTANT: MNE ANNOTATIONS AND OUTPUTS
--------------------------------------
Saved in outputs/eeg/manual_annotations/sub###/:
  <recording>_ALL_span1-annot.fif
  <recording>_ALL_span1_intervals.csv
  <recording>_AF8_span1-annot.fif
  <recording>_AF8_span1_intervals.csv

The CSV includes label, affected_channels, display-relative onset/offset,
recording-relative onset/offset, source timestamps and reviewer_note.
Labels beginning BAD_ can be excluded by MNE processes configured with
reject_by_annotation; REVIEW_uncertain is NOT automatically excluded.

The annotation viewer uses nominal FS=256 sample positions; actual source
CSV timestamp spacing may be rounded. Use exported recording_start_s and
recording_end_s when aligning with the audit windows. Timestamp gaps split
recordings into continuous spans. The display assumes raw values are in
microvolts; confirm original device units before interpreting magnitude.

The script DOES NOT automatically integrate annotated BAD intervals into
EEG_Audit.py. Final validated AF7/AF8 alpha/beta and log(beta/alpha)
measurements require a later annotation-aware spectral recalculation and
sensitivity review. Expert review is needed for uncertain artifact classes.

DISPLAY FIX — OCTOBER 2026
===========================
If all four traces are black/thick or extend beyond their rows, the visual display
scale is too small for the extremely large values in some channels. In subject
029 pre-VR, exact 0/1450 readings occur in 39.3% of TP9 and 35.9% of TP10
samples, versus 0.3% AF7 and 0.9% AF8. These are suspicious apparent
measurement-boundary values (not independently confirmed as clipping).

Use the updated script (default --scale-uv 1600, --duration 5):
  python3 scripts/eeg/EEG_Manual_Review.py --subject 029 --phase pre_vr
For a more compressed overall four-channel view:
  python3 scripts/eeg/EEG_Manual_Review.py --subject 029 --phase pre_vr --scale-uv 2400
For a focused frontal view:
  python3 scripts/eeg/EEG_Manual_Review.py --subject 029 --phase pre_vr --channel AF7 --scale-uv 600
  python3 scripts/eeg/EEG_Manual_Review.py --subject 029 --phase pre_vr --channel AF8 --scale-uv 600

Changing --scale-uv adjusts the DISPLAY ONLY. It neither alters source raw EEG
samples nor repairs saturated signals. Large swings may still indicate genuine
acquisition problems. Do not confuse an MNE visual display-scale problem with
a failed recording: inspect channel distributions and zoomed waveforms.

Open EEG_Artifact_Visual_Examples.png for eight SCHEMATIC examples (not real EEG).
Open EEG_029_Real_Trace_Example.png for actual example from subject 029.
Flatline = near-constant series, possible dropout (BAD_flatline).
Clipping = flat-top or boundary saturation (BAD_clipping_suspected).
Step = sudden persistent level shift (BAD_contact_step or REVIEW_uncertain).
Ocular = large slow smooth frontal deflection (BAD_ocular_suspected / REVIEW_uncertain).
Muscle = burst of rapid dense irregular fluctuations (BAD_muscle_suspected / REVIEW_uncertain).
Spike = sharp transient (BAD_contact_step / REVIEW_uncertain).
Electrical noise = persistent unusually regular oscillation (BAD_electrical_noise if severe).
Unremarkable looking signal = do not label BAD solely because it fluctuates.

Artifact classes are interpretations, not confirmed physiological sources.
Review simultaneous TP9/AF7/AF8/TP10 timing, retaining AF7/AF8 as the primary
spectral channels. Annotations labeled BAD_ are rejection decisions; use
REVIEW_uncertain when source or eligibility is unresolved. NEVER call the
illustrative examples actual participant data.
