MAJOR FINDINGS: MINORS COMPARED WITH OLDER SUBJECTS
==================================================

Sample
------
The comparison includes 6 minor subjects and 12 older subjects. Session 2
has 5 minors and 11 older subjects because subjects 027 and 014 are missing
Session 2, respectively. Results are exploratory because the groups are small.

Learning performance
--------------------
Session 1 average learning-rate k was 0.333 for minors and 0.661 for older subjects (minor-minus-older difference -0.328, Hedges' g = -0.542, p = .192).
Session 2 average learning-rate k was 0.161 for minors and 0.541 for older subjects (difference -0.380, Hedges' g = -0.664, p = .104). Lower k indicates a slower fitted approach to the learning asymptote.
Despite lower fitted k values, minors had higher mean accuracy: Session 1 0.653 versus 0.573 (difference 0.080, p = .314), and Session 2 0.642 versus 0.538 (difference 0.104, p = .340). This is possible because fitted learning speed and overall accuracy measure different features.
Average linear slope was 0.0099 for minors versus 0.0038 for older subjects in Session 1, and 0.0066 versus 0.0079 in Session 2.

Win-stay and lose-switch strategy use
-------------------------------------
Strategy-use counts below define use as a conditional probability of at least 0.50; the mean probabilities are also reported so the threshold does not hide variation.
Session 1 win-stay probability, minors: mean 0.690; 4/6 (66.7%) at or above 0.50.
Session 1 win-stay probability, older: mean 0.651; 8/12 (66.7%) at or above 0.50.
Session 1 lose-switch probability, minors: mean 0.516; 3/6 (50.0%) at or above 0.50.
Session 1 lose-switch probability, older: mean 0.529; 7/12 (58.3%) at or above 0.50.
Session 2 win-stay probability, minors: mean 0.711; 4/5 (80.0%) at or above 0.50.
Session 2 win-stay probability, older: mean 0.567; 8/11 (72.7%) at or above 0.50.
Session 2 lose-switch probability, minors: mean 0.458; 2/5 (40.0%) at or above 0.50.
Session 2 lose-switch probability, older: mean 0.491; 7/11 (63.6%) at or above 0.50.

Heart-rate reactivity
---------------------
Math reactivity: minors 7.466 bpm, older subjects 9.158 bpm; difference -1.692 bpm, p = .604.
Speech reactivity: minors 9.398 bpm, older subjects 7.178 bpm; difference 2.220 bpm, p = .773.
Preparation reactivity: minors 7.384 bpm, older subjects 11.051 bpm; difference -3.667 bpm, p = .415.

Statistical conclusion
----------------------
None of the full set of 160 outcome comparisons was significant after
Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction. Among the focused key
results, the smallest unadjusted p-value was for learning rate k (Session 2; p = .104, FDR-adjusted p = 0.937). These data show descriptive group
differences, not reliable evidence of age-group effects. Larger groups are
needed for stable effect estimates and confirmatory inference.

Reading the figures
-------------------
Bars show group means, error bars show standard errors, and dots show individual
subjects. The standardized-effect figure shows Hedges' g as minor minus older;
positive values favor higher values among minors. It intentionally does not show
confidence intervals, so it should be used as a descriptive overview only.
