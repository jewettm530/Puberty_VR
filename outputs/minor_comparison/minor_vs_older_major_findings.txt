MAJOR FINDINGS: MINORS COMPARED WITH OLDER SUBJECTS
==================================================

Sample
------
The comparison includes 8 minor subjects and 12 older subjects.
Session 2 includes 7 minors and 11 older subjects.
Results remain exploratory, particularly when group sample sizes are small.

Learning performance
--------------------
Session 1 average learning-rate k was 0.302 for minors and 0.661 for older subjects (minor-minus-older difference -0.359, Hedges' g = -0.630, p = .119).
Session 2 average learning-rate k was 0.199 for minors and 0.541 for older subjects (difference -0.343, Hedges' g = -0.632, p = .128). Lower k indicates a slower fitted approach to the learning asymptote.
Despite lower fitted k values, minors had higher mean accuracy: Session 1 0.640 versus 0.573 (difference 0.066, p = .315), and Session 2 0.621 versus 0.538 (difference 0.083, p = .306). This is possible because fitted learning speed and overall accuracy measure different features.
Average linear slope was 0.0109 for minors versus 0.0038 for older subjects in Session 1, and 0.0058 versus 0.0079 in Session 2.

Win-stay and lose-switch strategy use
-------------------------------------
Strategy-use counts below define use as a conditional probability of at least 0.50; the mean probabilities are also reported so the threshold does not hide variation.
Session 1 win-stay probability, minors: mean 0.679; 6/8 (75.0%) at or above 0.50.
Session 1 win-stay probability, older: mean 0.651; 8/12 (66.7%) at or above 0.50.
Session 1 lose-switch probability, minors: mean 0.497; 3/8 (37.5%) at or above 0.50.
Session 1 lose-switch probability, older: mean 0.529; 7/12 (58.3%) at or above 0.50.
Session 2 win-stay probability, minors: mean 0.694; 6/7 (85.7%) at or above 0.50.
Session 2 win-stay probability, older: mean 0.567; 8/11 (72.7%) at or above 0.50.
Session 2 lose-switch probability, minors: mean 0.477; 4/7 (57.1%) at or above 0.50.
Session 2 lose-switch probability, older: mean 0.491; 7/11 (63.6%) at or above 0.50.

Heart-rate reactivity
---------------------
Math reactivity: minors 7.035 bpm, older subjects 9.158 bpm; difference -2.123 bpm, p = .433.
Speech reactivity: minors 9.835 bpm, older subjects 7.178 bpm; difference 2.657 bpm, p = .642.
Preparation reactivity: minors 10.527 bpm, older subjects 11.051 bpm; difference -0.524 bpm, p = .940.

Statistical conclusion
----------------------
0 of 160 valid outcome comparisons were significant after
Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction. Among the focused key
results, the smallest unadjusted p-value was for change in linear slope (Session 2 minus Session 1; p = .051, FDR-adjusted p = 0.953). These exploratory differences require
careful interpretation and replication; larger groups improve the stability
of effect estimates and support confirmatory inference.

Reading the figures
-------------------
Bars show group means, error bars show standard errors, and dots show individual
subjects. The standardized-effect figure shows Hedges' g as minor minus older;
positive values favor higher values among minors. It intentionally does not show
confidence intervals, so it should be used as a descriptive overview only.
