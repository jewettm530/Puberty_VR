MAJOR FINDINGS: MINORS COMPARED WITH OLDER SUBJECTS
==================================================

Sample
------
The comparison includes 7 minor subjects and 25 older subjects.
Session 2 includes 6 minors and 20 older subjects.
Results remain exploratory, particularly when group sample sizes are small.

Learning performance
--------------------
Session 1 average learning-rate k was 0.318 for minors and 0.526 for older subjects (minor-minus-older difference -0.208, Hedges' g = -0.400, p = .233).
Session 2 average learning-rate k was 0.229 for minors and 0.439 for older subjects (difference -0.210, Hedges' g = -0.410, p = .223). Lower k indicates a slower fitted approach to the learning asymptote.
Despite lower fitted k values, minors had higher mean accuracy: Session 1 0.659 versus 0.592 (difference 0.067, p = .288), and Session 2 0.634 versus 0.586 (difference 0.048, p = .580). This is possible because fitted learning speed and overall accuracy measure different features.
Average linear slope was 0.0113 for minors versus 0.0080 for older subjects in Session 1, and 0.0055 versus 0.0075 in Session 2.

Win-stay and lose-switch strategy use
-------------------------------------
Strategy-use counts below define use as a conditional probability of at least 0.50; the mean probabilities are also reported so the threshold does not hide variation.
Session 1 win-stay probability, minors: mean 0.704; 5/7 (71.4%) at or above 0.50.
Session 1 win-stay probability, older: mean 0.673; 20/25 (80.0%) at or above 0.50.
Session 1 lose-switch probability, minors: mean 0.501; 3/7 (42.9%) at or above 0.50.
Session 1 lose-switch probability, older: mean 0.528; 16/25 (64.0%) at or above 0.50.
Session 2 win-stay probability, minors: mean 0.714; 5/6 (83.3%) at or above 0.50.
Session 2 win-stay probability, older: mean 0.634; 15/20 (75.0%) at or above 0.50.
Session 2 lose-switch probability, minors: mean 0.470; 3/6 (50.0%) at or above 0.50.
Session 2 lose-switch probability, older: mean 0.480; 14/20 (70.0%) at or above 0.50.

Heart-rate reactivity
---------------------
Math reactivity: minors 7.389 bpm, older subjects 8.832 bpm; difference -1.443 bpm, p = .606.
Speech reactivity: minors 10.947 bpm, older subjects 6.869 bpm; difference 4.078 bpm, p = .535.
Preparation reactivity: minors 13.330 bpm, older subjects 9.840 bpm; difference 3.490 bpm, p = .640.

Statistical conclusion
----------------------
0 of 160 valid outcome comparisons were significant after
Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction. Among the focused key
results, the smallest unadjusted p-value was for change in linear slope (Session 2 minus Session 1; p = .174, FDR-adjusted p = 0.998). These exploratory differences require
careful interpretation and replication; larger groups improve the stability
of effect estimates and support confirmatory inference.

Reading the figures
-------------------
Bars show group means, error bars show standard errors, and dots show individual
subjects. The standardized-effect figure shows Hedges' g as minor minus older;
positive values favor higher values among minors. It intentionally does not show
confidence intervals, so it should be used as a descriptive overview only.
